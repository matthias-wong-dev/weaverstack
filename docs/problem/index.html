The problem

Fabric is powerful. It's also remarkably easy to build a mess.

Fabric gives you enormous freedom. A notebook here, a Warehouse query there, a semantic model and a few Reports.

You can deliver something useful very quickly.

The trouble starts when those pieces need to work together. And then keep working as the data changes, the project grows and people begin depending on it.

Everything works. Until it needs to work together.

A Python script loads some files. A Spark SQL transformation produces a Delta Table. A Warehouse query consumes it. A semantic model needs refreshing. A Report depends on that model.

None of these is particularly difficult on its own.

But now you need to worry about dependencies, execution order, data availability and what happens when something fails halfway through.

Before long, the engineering around your code becomes more complicated than the code itself.

And that's just one data pipeline.

Power BI brings its own problems

Your first semantic model is straightforward.

Then you build another.

The naming conventions differ. Measures are formatted differently. Relationships and hidden columns aren't consistent. A business rule changes, and now you have to remember everywhere it was implemented.

What if you want several variations of the same model, with shared foundations but different measures or audiences?

Without a systematic approach, every variation becomes another model to maintain.

And a successful refresh doesn't tell you whether the calculations are right.

Reports introduce another layer of uncertainty.

Publish a Report to another workspace. Which semantic model is it connected to? Does its name match? Did you remember to change the connection? Did you select the right model?

It's surprisingly easy to deploy something that appears successful but points to the wrong place.

And the more models and Reports you have, the harder it becomes to keep them consistent.

Now try moving everything between environments

Development works. Now you need test and production.

Different workspaces. Different Lakehouse and Warehouse names. Different semantic-model targets, Reports and source connections.

Suddenly your code is full of variables, substitutions and environment-specific exceptions.

Or you're maintaining copies of things that should have remained identical.

Every deployment becomes another opportunity to miss a reference, overwrite something or connect a Report to the wrong model.

The project hasn't changed. Why should deploying it somewhere else be so difficult?

And someone still has to do the serious engineering

Even when all the pieces are connected, you haven't finished.

A production data estate needs incremental processing, bookmarks, change detection, deletes, logging, statistics, dependency scheduling, concurrency, fault tolerance and reliable recovery.

You need to know what succeeded, what failed, what was skipped and what needs to run again.

You need tests that establish whether the data is actually correct, not merely whether the pipeline completed.

And you need all of this to continue working as the estate grows.

These are substantial engineering problems. Getting them right takes time and experience.

But your team was hired to deliver data products, not reinvent a data engineering framework for Fabric.

The bigger problem is what happens when you succeed

A small solution becomes useful. More people depend on it. More sources are added. More models and Reports appear.

What started as a few perfectly reasonable notebooks and queries becomes an increasingly complicated collection of dependencies, scripts and deployment procedures.

Eventually, making a small change feels risky.

And instead of delivering new capabilities, your team is spending its time maintaining the engineering it built along the way.

Keep building what matters. Weaver handles the engineering around it.

Weaver brings Lakehouse, Warehouse, semantic-model and Report development into one framework.

Keep writing the Python, SQL and native Power BI files your team already knows.

Weaver handles the dependencies, generated load procedures, incremental state, validations, deployment mappings and operational machinery that would otherwise have to be designed and maintained separately.

Start with one Table. Add another. Connect a Warehouse. Deploy a semantic model. Publish Reports to different environments.

The same engineering framework grows with you.

You get Fabric's freedom without having to build and maintain all the machinery needed to keep it under control.
